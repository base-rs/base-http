Publish available `io_uring` completions immediately after a successful nonblocking submission, so completed operations and their wakeups reach the executor before it resumes runnable work.

The reactor already collects a completion snapshot at the start of a turn and after a blocking wait. Its submit-only path previously returned without collecting the CQEs made available by submission, delaying publication until another turn. Reuse the existing snapshot collector on this path; application futures still run through the executor, and the change adds no ring-submission syscall, persistent state or public API. Regression coverage checks immediate delivery, notification errors and duplicate issuance/completion wakeups; notification fixtures allow completions to retire during submission.

On Hana, five interleaved paired raw-server rounds at the **same 240,000 offered requests/s**, with 64 connections, reduce median RTT p50 from **127.6 to 74.8 µs**. Within-round median changes are **−41.47% for p50** and **−33.94% for mean RTT**, with every pair improving. **RTT p99 is essentially unchanged**: 364.3 to 357.4 µs across per-run medians, and a −0.70% paired median with mixed signs. P99 measured from scheduled arrival, including waiting behind earlier requests, improves in four of five pairs (−54.80% paired median), with substantial variation. All 48 million responses including warmup validate; both Base variants achieve essentially identical request rates.

A **separate unrestricted throughput comparison** improves throughput by **2.74%** and reduces CPU/request by **0.95%** at 64 connections, all five pairs favorable. One-connection CPU/request improves by 3.86%. These controls bypass HTTP framing and do not establish full HTTP, multicore or uniformly better tail-latency performance. The measured binaries contain no diagnostic counters.
